    Water viscosity is taken from the mean fluid temperature. Glycol, if selected, is applied as a ratio on that water figure.

    Mean temperature is outside 0–100 °C. Viscosity is calculated at the nearest limit of that range.

    Ethylene Glycol = Mono Ethylene Glycol (MEG). Propylene is different (safer, higher viscosity).
    Glycol is applied as a ratio on water µ / ρ / cp — not a full glycol viscosity correlation. Use a manual override for design.

    In Flow & Return mode the temperature difference is calculated automatically from the two temperatures you enter.

    Bare straight pipe only — no fittings, no length. For checking an external calculator. This does not write into the tables above.

    Notes & Assumptions

    Straight pipe only at the selected Pa/m limit (CIBSE typical upper guidance 300 Pa/m). Add fittings, valves, strainers and elevation separately.

    Pipe tables: Tru-Bore Metric (ID = DN) and carbon steel BS EN 10255 Medium (DN100 = 105.3 mm).
    Darcy–Weisbach with Colebrook–White via Swamee–Jain; laminar f = 64/Re below Re 2300.
    Water viscosity is temperature-corrected at the mean operating temperature. Density and specific heat from the toolchain polynomial fits at that same temperature.
    Velocities highlighted in amber when > 3 m/s (check noise / erosion).
    Ethylene Glycol = Mono Ethylene Glycol (MEG). Propylene glycol is different and more viscous.
